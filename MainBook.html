Roasting: Times & Temperatures

HOW TO USE THIS BOOK

All recipes in this volume are written for gluten-free ingredients and flour, so that qualification is not repeated throughout. Measurements are easy-to-remember starting points; adjust freely when doing so will not compromise food safety.

Basic method

Spread the ingredients you are roasting in a single layer on a metal baking sheet. Drizzle with cooking oil and toss to coat, then add salt and spices to taste. The Blend is a pretty good first choice; some combination of salt, pepper, and garlic is usually helpful. Put the sheet in a preheated oven and cook. Halfway through, stir or flip the food, and add a little more oil if it looks dry. Return it to the oven to finish. Remove it when it looks done and feels tender—the times below are guides, not laws. Stir again, taste, and add more salt or spices if needed. For an extra zest, squeeze fresh lemon over the finished food.

Air fryer variation

Use the same oil and seasoning, but put the food in the basket in a loose, mostly single layer. Preheat if your model recommends it. Shake or turn halfway through; work in batches when necessary.

Quick rules

Dry washed vegetables before roasting; avoid crowding; cut similar-sized pieces. Bare, lightly oiled metal gives the strongest browning. Start checking at the low end of each time range, especially with an air fryer or convection oven.

Two useful exceptions

For best browning, leave Brussels sprouts cut-side down for much of the oven roast rather than stirring repeatedly. Thinly sliced or minced fresh garlic can burn at 425°F; add it near the end, or use garlic powder at the start. Whole-head garlic follows the separate foil-wrapped method in the table.

Change Your Life Chicken

500 FOR 50 · CHICKEN + ANY VEGETABLES YOU LIKE

OVEN TEMPERATUREROAST TIMEPREP TIME
500°F50 MIN~10 MIN

Ingredients

1–2 bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs per adult; about 2 handfuls of vegetables per person (entirely your choice); olive oil, salt, and black pepper.

Instructions

  1. Heat the oven to 500°F. Line a rimmed metal sheet pan with heavy-duty foil. Do not use parchment at this temperature.
  2. Choose whatever vegetables you want. Cut into bite-sized pieces, toss with olive oil, salt, and pepper, and spread in a single layer. Cut slow-cooking vegetables smaller; add delicate ones later if needed.
  3. Season the chicken generously on both sides, including underneath the skin. Put the skin back in place and pat the outside dry.
  4. Lay the thighs skin-side up on the vegetables. Roast about 50 minutes, until the skin is crisp and the thickest part of the chicken reaches at least 165°F (without touching the bone).
  5. Rest a few minutes, then serve the chicken with the vegetables and their pan juices.

VEGETABLES ARE YOUR CHOICE

The recipe works with whatever vegetables you enjoy. Hearty vegetables (such as potatoes or carrots) can roast the whole time; quick-cooking ones (such as asparagus, zucchini, or broccoli) may do better added during the last 10–20 minutes. Adjust by sight and tenderness rather than following a fixed vegetable list.

WHY IT WORKS

Fat and juices from the skin-on chicken season the vegetables; the hot oven crisps the exposed chicken skin. A complete meal on one pan.

Instant Pot: Everyday Recipes

Short recipes. Big timing labels. Easy numbers to remember.

First: What do the times mean?

LABELMEANING

TIMING

Heating up and venting can add 10–25+ minutes, depending on the food and pot. “10+ min” means wait at least 10, then you may vent or continue waiting.

Hard-Boiled Eggs

THE 5–5–5 METHOD

COOK · HIGH PRESSURENATURAL RELEASEICE BATH
5 MIN5 MIN5 MIN

Ingredients

Eggs (1–12), 2 cups water, ice.

Instructions

  1. Add water. Place eggs on the trivet/rack, raised above the water and away from the metal sides.
  2. Seal; pressure cook on HIGH for 5 minutes.
  3. Wait 5 minutes, then vent any remaining pressure. Transfer eggs to an ice-water bath for 5 minutes.

DON’T FORGET

Start assembling the ice bath the moment natural release begins. Otherwise, you may forget the eggs are still cooking!

Rice

WHITE OR BROWN; YOUR HALF-INCH METHOD

RICECOOK · HIGHNATURAL RELEASE
White rice4 MIN10+ MIN
Brown rice20 MIN10+ MIN

Ingredients

2–4 cups rice, water, salt, ½ stick butter (optional).

Instructions

  1. Rinse rice repeatedly in the pot, pouring off cloudy water until the rinse is fairly clear.
  2. Level the drained rice. Add fresh water until it stands about ½ inch above the rice.
  3. Add salt to taste and optional butter. Use Rice for white rice if available; otherwise choose HIGH and the cook time above.
  4. Wait at least 10 minutes before venting. It can sit longer on Keep Warm until dinner.
  5. As soon as you open the pot, fluff and scrape/stir the bottom to prevent sticking.

WATER OPTION

Measured alternative: white rice = 1 cup water per 1 cup dry rice. For brown rice, use about 1–1¼ cups water per cup of dry rice (start with 1¼ for softer rice). Measure before rinsing; drain well.

BROWN RICE

Brown rice needs the longer HIGH-pressure setting. A standard Rice button may be calibrated for white rice. Keep cooked rice hot on Keep Warm, or refrigerate promptly.

“Baked” Potatoes

SOFT POTATOES; NO OVEN NEEDED

COOK · HIGH PRESSURENATURAL RELEASE
20 MIN10 MIN

Ingredients

2–4 medium russet potatoes, 2 cups water; butter, salt, and toppings.

Instructions

  1. Scrub potatoes and prick with a fork. Put water in the pot and potatoes on a trivet (not submerged).
  2. Pressure cook on HIGH for 20 minutes; wait 10 minutes, then vent remaining pressure.
  3. Check with a fork. Split open and fluff; add toppings.

SIZE

Very large potatoes: try 25–30 minutes. Pressure cooking does not crisp the skins; for that, oil and finish at 425°F in the oven for about 10 minutes.

Broths

Basic Chicken Broth

LEFTOVER CARCASS = BROTH

COOK · HIGH PRESSURENATURAL RELEASE
45 MIN20 MIN

Ingredients

1 cooked chicken carcass (or 1–2 lb bony chicken pieces), 1 onion, 2 carrots, 2–3 garlic cloves, 6–8 cups water; salt and pepper.

Instructions

  1. Put bones/chicken and roughly chopped vegetables in the pot. Add water to cover without passing the fill line.
  2. Pressure cook on HIGH for 45 minutes. Natural release for at least 20 minutes; ideally let pressure finish dropping on its own.
  3. Strain. Add salt to taste. Refrigerate promptly in shallow containers or freeze.

CHICKEN IS OPTIONAL

For true chicken broth, leftover bones/carcass are enough—no chicken meat needed. For a good completely chicken-free broth, follow the Vegetable & Mushroom Broth recipe directly below. For richer bone stock, cook 90–120 minutes on HIGH.

Vegetable & Mushroom Broth

SAVORY; NO CHICKEN NEEDED

COOK · HIGH PRESSURENATURAL RELEASE
20 MIN20 MIN

Ingredients

1 onion, 2 carrots, 1–2 cups mushrooms, 3 garlic cloves, 6–8 cups water; pepper, bay leaf, herbs, salt.

Instructions

  1. Roughly chop everything. Optional: brown the onion and mushrooms on Sauté for a deeper flavor.
  2. Add water and seasonings. Pressure cook on HIGH for 20 minutes.
  3. Natural release 20 minutes; vent only if needed. Strain and salt to taste. Chill or freeze promptly.

WHY IT WORKS

Mushrooms give this broth a meaty, savory flavor. Use in rice, soups, sauces, or anywhere chicken broth would be convenient.

Five Easy Favorites

1. Weeknight Chili

COOK · HIGH PRESSURENATURAL RELEASE
10 MIN10 MIN

Ingredients

1 lb ground beef, 1 chopped onion, 2 cans beans (drained), 2 cans diced tomatoes, 2 cups broth, 2 tbsp chili powder, 1 tsp cumin, salt.

Instructions

  1. Sauté beef and onion until beef is browned. Drain excess fat if needed.
  2. Add broth and scrape the bottom clean. Add beans and spices; pour tomatoes on top without stirring them into the bottom.
  3. Pressure cook HIGH 10 minutes; natural release 10 minutes, then vent. Stir and season. If thin, simmer on Sauté a few minutes.

2. Pot Roast

CHUCK ROAST THAT FALLS APART

COOK · HIGH PRESSURENATURAL RELEASE
60 MIN20 MIN

Ingredients

2½–3 lb beef chuck roast, 1 onion, 2 cups beef broth, salt, pepper, garlic.

Instructions

  1. Season roast generously. Optional: brown both sides with Sauté for extra flavor.
  2. Add broth and scrape up browned bits. Add quartered onion and roast.
  3. Pressure cook HIGH for 60 minutes; natural release 20 minutes, then vent. Slice or shred when fork-tender.

VEGETABLES

Carrots and potatoes will get extremely soft if cooked with the roast for the full hour. Cook them separately, or add them afterward for a short second pressure cycle.

3. Pulled Pork

CHUNK IT; SHRED IT; SAUCE IT

COOK · HIGH PRESSURENATURAL RELEASE
45 MIN15 MIN

Ingredients

3 lb pork shoulder (cut into large 2–3-in. chunks), 2 cups broth, salt, pepper, garlic, favorite barbecue sauce (optional).

Instructions

  1. Season pork chunks. Add broth and pork to pot.
  2. Pressure cook HIGH for 45 minutes; natural release 15 minutes, then vent.
  3. Shred with forks. Drain or reduce liquid as desired; toss with barbecue sauce at the end.

TEXTURE

Still too firm to shred? Cook 10 more minutes at HIGH pressure. Add thick barbecue sauce AFTER pressure cooking to avoid scorching.

4. Mashed Potatoes

NO NEED TO BABYSIT THE BOIL

COOK · HIGH PRESSURENATURAL RELEASE
8 MIN0 MIN · VENT

Ingredients

2 lb potatoes, peeled or not, cut into 1-in. chunks; 2 cups water, ½ stick butter, ½ cup milk, salt.

Instructions

  1. Put potato chunks and water in the pot; add a pinch of salt.
  2. Pressure cook HIGH 8 minutes. Vent immediately at the end (stop briefly if starchy liquid sputters).
  3. Drain well. Mash with butter and milk; add salt to taste.

OPTION

Add more milk gradually if needed. The measurements are deliberately easy to remember.

5. No-Soak Black Beans

COOK · HIGH PRESSURENATURAL RELEASE
40 MIN20 MIN

Ingredients

1 lb dried black beans, 6 cups water, 1 tsp salt; optional onion, garlic, cumin.

Instructions

  1. Rinse and pick over the beans. Add beans, water, salt, and any seasonings. Do not soak.
  2. Pressure cook HIGH for 40 minutes; natural release 20 minutes, then vent.
  3. Check tenderness, salt to taste, and drain excess liquid if desired.

BEANS

Older beans may need 10–15 minutes more pressure time. Never fill above the ½ line for expanding beans.

Everyday Recipes

Simple quantities, flexible measurements, and minimal fuss.

Dad's Mashed Potatoes

5 POUNDS • 1 STICK OF BUTTER • SEASON GENEROUSLY

BOIL TIMEREADY WHEN
20–30 MINFALLING APART

Ingredients

5 lb potatoes; 1 stick butter; milk, sour cream, and/or plain Greek yogurt; salt, pepper, and garlic. Optional: Kinder’s The Blend or Buttery Garlic and Herb.

Instructions

  1. Peel potatoes and cut each into roughly 4 pieces. Add to a pot of boiling water.
  2. Boil about 20–30 minutes after the water returns to a boil, until the potatoes fall apart easily.
  3. Remove from heat and drain. Leaving a little water at the bottom is fine. Mash thoroughly.
  4. Add the full stick of butter. Mash in milk, sour cream, and/or Greek yogurt until as smooth and creamy as you want.
  5. Season generously with salt, pepper, garlic, and/or either Kinder’s blend. Taste and adjust repeatedly; you will probably need more seasoning than you think.

TOO SALTY?

A little milk may help, but additional unsalted potatoes are the better fix for substantial oversalting.

DAIRY-FREE VARIATION

Omit butter and dairy; use unsweetened, unflavored coconut milk instead. Do not use Kinder’s Buttery Garlic and Herb.

Easy Pancakes and Waffles

REMEMBER THE FOURS; WAFFLES GET BUTTER

PANCAKESWAFFLES
2–3 MIN / SIDE3–5 MIN

Ingredients

4 eggs; 4 cups gluten-free 1:1 baking flour; 4 cups milk; 4 tsp baking powder; 2 tsp salt; optional splash of vanilla and 2–4 tbsp sugar. Waffles only: 4 tbsp melted butter.

Instructions

  1. Mix dry ingredients in one bowl. Beat the eggs well in another bowl; combine with milk and vanilla.
  2. Mix the wet and dry ingredients together. For waffles only, stir in melted butter.
  3. Let the batter rest 5–10 minutes. Cook pancakes on a greased medium-heat skillet, or use a greased waffle maker.

DON’T OVERTHINK IT

These proportions are very flexible. Too thick? Add milk. Too thin? Add flour. Want more body or fluff? Add an egg. Whatever works well for you is great.

Easy Skillet Hash Browns

COARSE GRATER • FLAT CAKE • GOLDEN ON BOTH SIDES

Ingredients

1 potato (fresh or previously baked); butter, oil, or bacon grease; salt, pepper, and other seasonings as desired.

SKILLET TIME About 5–7 minutes on the first side and 4–6 minutes on the second, or until golden brown and the center is cooked through.

Instructions

  1. Add butter, oil, or bacon grease to a skillet over medium heat. Shred the potato using the coarse holes of a cheese grater (directly into the skillet or into a bowl first).
  2. Spread into a thin, fairly solid cake and press gently with a spatula. Add salt, pepper, or any other seasonings you like.
  3. Cook without stirring until the underside looks lightly crispy and golden at the edges. Flip carefully; it is fine if the cake breaks apart.
  4. Salt the other side. If it looks dry, add more butter or oil around the edges or over the top. Cook until golden brown and serve.

FRESH OR BAKED? Fresh potatoes are my preference for a firmer, crispier result. Previously baked potatoes work too, but make softer hash browns; since they are already cooked, judge by the browning rather than the center.

OPTIONAL CRISPIER RESULT If fresh potato shreds seem especially wet, pat them dry before frying.

Sheet-Pan Bacon, Eggs & Potatoes

425°F • POTATOES + BACON: 20–25 MIN • EGGS + CHEESE: 8–14 MIN

Ingredients

Frozen hash browns or freshly grated potatoes; strips of bacon; eggs; shredded cheese; salt and pepper (or Kinder’s The Blend). Optional: a little butter or oil.

Instructions

  1. Heat oven to 425°F. Spread potatoes in a fairly thin layer on a rimmed metal sheet pan. Season with salt, pepper, or The Blend.
  2. Lay bacon strips evenly over the potatoes, leaving roughly 1½–2 bacon-widths between strips.
  3. Bake about 20–25 minutes, until the potatoes are beginning to brown and the bacon is nearly cooked.
  4. Remove the pan. Make shallow spaces in the potatoes to help the eggs stay put. Crack eggs over the top wherever you like, season them, and sprinkle with as much cheese as you want.
  5. Return to the oven for about 8–14 minutes, until the potatoes and bacon are done and the eggs reach your preferred doneness. For the food-safety standard, cook until the whites and yolks are firm.

ALL-AT-ONCE OPTION You can add eggs and cheese at the beginning and bake everything for about 30 minutes. It is simpler but usually makes the eggs much firmer; the two-stage method gives more control.

USE WHAT YOU HAVE Frozen hash browns or freshly grated potatoes both work. If the potato layer looks dry, add a little oil or butter. Freshly grated potatoes brown better if patted dry.

Easy Egg Salad

HARD-BOILED EGGS + A GLOP OF MAYO (OR MUSTARD)

Ingredients

Hard-boiled eggs (one or more); a glop of mayonnaise. For a lighter version, use mustard instead.

Instructions

  1. Peel the hard-boiled eggs and put them in a bowl with a glop of mayonnaise.
  2. Smash the eggs into the mayonnaise with a fork, mixing until it is as smooth or chunky as you like.

LOWER-CALORIE OPTION

Use mustard instead of mayonnaise. It will be tangier and less creamy, but still good. Serve right away or refrigerate promptly.

Creamy Tuscan Chicken

ABOUT 15 MIN PREP • 30–35 MIN COOK • CHICKEN TO 165°F

Ingredients

2 large boneless, skinless chicken breasts; 1 small onion; oil-packed sun-dried tomatoes (a small handful or about ½ cup); 1 bag fresh spinach; about 1 cup heavy cream; a handful of grated Parmesan; olive oil. Seasonings: salt, pepper, garlic, and Italian seasoning—all to taste.

Instructions

  1. Cut each chicken breast horizontally into two thinner pieces. Season with salt and pepper. Sear in an oiled skillet over medium-high heat about 3–5 minutes per side, until golden; set aside (the centers may still be raw).
  2. Lower heat to medium. In the same skillet, soften chopped onion for a few minutes. Stir in garlic, sliced sun-dried tomatoes, and Italian seasoning; then add spinach and cook until wilted.
  3. Add cream and Parmesan. Stir, scraping up the browned bits, and bring to a gentle simmer. Add more cream if you want extra sauce.
  4. Return chicken and its juices to the pan. Simmer until the thickest part reaches 165°F, usually another 4–7 minutes for thin pieces. Taste the sauce and adjust the seasonings.

EASY VARIATIONS Use chicken thighs instead of breasts (adjust the cooking time), or add extra spinach, tomatoes, or cream. Serve over rice, potatoes, or pasta.

Man-Pleasing Chicken (Maple-Dijon)

OVEN 450°F • BAKE ABOUT 30–40 MIN • CHICKEN TO 165°F

Ingredients: About 1½ lb boneless, skinless chicken thighs; ½ cup Dijon mustard; ¼ cup pure maple syrup; 1 tbsp rice wine vinegar (optional); salt and pepper. Optional: rosemary or green onions.

Instructions

  1. Preheat oven to 450°F. Place chicken in a lightly greased or foil-lined baking dish and season with salt and pepper.
  2. Mix Dijon mustard and maple syrup. Add vinegar if using. Pour over the chicken and turn to coat well.
  3. Bake about 30–40 minutes, spooning sauce over the chicken halfway through, until the thickest part reaches 165°F.
  4. Rest 5 minutes. Spoon some pan sauce over the chicken and add rosemary or green onions if you like.

EASY RATIO 2 parts Dijon mustard to 1 part maple syrup. If you prefer a sweeter glaze, use a little more maple syrup.

VINEGAR IS OPTIONAL It adds tang to balance the maple syrup, but the mustard already provides acidity. Leave it out, or substitute apple cider vinegar, white wine vinegar, or a squeeze of lemon.

NOODLES

Use egg noodles specifically made without wheat, or another pasta you like. Follow the package cooking time: noodle styles vary, especially wheat-free varieties.

Buttered Egg Noodles (Optional Chicken)

JUST NOODLES, BUTTER, SALT, AND WHATEVER ELSE SOUNDS GOOD

NOODLE COOK TIMEFINISH IN PAN
PACKAGE TIME1–2 MIN

Ingredients

1 lb gluten-free egg noodles; 4 tbsp butter (½ stick); salt. Optional: pepper, garlic, parsley, Parmesan, or 1–2 cups cooked sliced chicken.

Instructions

  1. Boil noodles in salted water according to the package directions. Save ½ cup cooking water; drain.
  2. Toss warm noodles with butter and salt. Add a splash of the reserved water if needed to keep everything silky.
  3. Add garlic or other seasonings to taste, plus cooked sliced chicken if desired. Toss and serve.

Quick Tomato Pasta

SAME IDEA, WITH CHOPPED TOMATOES

NOODLE COOK TIMETOMATO SIMMER
PACKAGE TIME5–10 MIN

Ingredients

1 lb gluten-free egg noodles or other pasta; 2 cups chopped tomatoes (or a 14–15 oz can); 4 tbsp butter or olive oil; salt, garlic, pepper. Optional: Italian herbs, ¼–½ cup milk, or cooked sliced chicken.

Instructions

  1. Cook noodles in salted water as directed. Reserve ½ cup pasta water; drain.
  2. Warm butter or oil in a skillet; add tomatoes, garlic, and seasonings. Simmer 5–10 minutes to soften and concentrate.
  3. Toss in noodles. Add a little reserved pasta water if dry, or simmer longer if too watery.
  4. For a creamy version, lower the heat and stir in a little milk at the end. Add cooked chicken if desired; taste and adjust.

THICKNESS

Milk adds creaminess, not automatic thickening. Simmer to reduce, or add a slurry of 1 tsp cornstarch mixed with 1 tbsp cold water. Stir into the gently simmering sauce until thickened. Keep heat low when adding milk to acidic tomatoes.

Anything-Goes Soup (Especially Leftovers)

4 CUPS BROTH + 2 CUPS VEGETABLES + LEFTOVER MEAT / BEANS

SIMMER (RAW VEGETABLES)ADD COOKED LEFTOVERS
15–25 MINLAST 5 MIN

Ingredients

4 cups broth (chicken or vegetable); about 2 cups chopped vegetables; 1–2 cups cooked meat or beans (optional); 1 cup cooked rice, pasta, or potatoes (optional); onion, garlic, salt, pepper, and other seasonings to taste.

Instructions

  1. Sauté chopped onion or garlic in butter or oil, if you feel like it. Otherwise, skip straight to the broth.
  2. Add broth and any firm RAW vegetables (carrots, potatoes, celery, etc.). Simmer 15–25 minutes until tender. Add tender vegetables toward the end.
  3. Add COOKED leftover meat, beans, and vegetables during the last 5 minutes, just long enough to heat thoroughly. Add cooked rice or pasta last so it does not turn mushy.
  4. Season to taste. Too thick? Add broth or water. Too thin? Simmer a little longer or add more leftovers. Finish with lemon, herbs, or cheese if you want.

LEFTOVER FORMULA

Broth + whatever vegetables you have + leftover cooked chicken, beef, beans, rice, or potatoes = soup. Smaller pieces heat faster. Start with 4 cups liquid and adjust freely.

FOOD SAFETY

Reheat leftovers to 165°F (bring soups to a boil when reheating). Raw meat must be cooked through separately or in the soup to its safe internal temperature. Use refrigerated leftovers within 3–4 days; refrigerate soup promptly in shallow containers.

Easy Sauerkraut

SLICE • PACK TIGHT • COVER WITH BRINE • WAIT

Ingredients: Cabbage; canning/pickling salt; water. Optional: other vegetables, herbs, garlic, or spices.

BRINE

2 tbsp pickling salt per 1 quart water (~3%).

Instructions

  1. Slice cabbage, thin or thick (each gives a different texture).
  2. Pack very tightly into clean fermentation jars, only about ⅔ full.
  3. Cover with mixed saltwater. Place a fermentation weight on top if needed to keep every piece below the brine; leave room for expansion.
  4. Fit an airlock lid, or use a lid that can safely vent (check regularly if manually burping). Keep at ~70–75°F, out of direct sun.
  5. Refrigerate when sufficiently sour. About 1 week can be quite crunchy and lightly fermented; a full ferment usually takes 3–4 weeks. Your preference is the rule for texture.

TRADITIONAL ALTERNATE

For a tested dry-salt kraut, use 3 tbsp pickling salt per 5 lb shredded cabbage. Pack until its own juices cover it; if needed, add brine made with 1½ tbsp salt per quart water.

REMINDER

Use a fermentation weight to hold cabbage under brine. Discard any batch that is slimy, moldy, or rotten-smelling. Refrigerate finished kraut; this is not a shelf-canning recipe.

Easy Fermented Dill Pickles

LIKE SAUERKRAUT: PACK • BRINE • WEIGHT • FERMENT

Ingredients: 2 lb small, fresh pickling cucumbers; 4 cups water; ¼ cup canning/pickling salt; 2 tbsp vinegar (5% acidity). Optional: 1 tbsp dill seed (or fresh dill), a garlic clove, peppercorns.

BRINE

4 cups water + ¼ cup pickling salt + 2 tbsp 5% vinegar.

Instructions

  1. Wash cucumbers. Cut a very thin slice off each blossom end (helps prevent mushy pickles). Leave small cucumbers whole for this tested recipe.
  2. Pack into a clean, roughly 2-quart fermentation container with dill and any optional spices, leaving headroom.
  3. Dissolve salt in water and vinegar; pour over cucumbers. Add a fermentation weight to keep all cucumbers submerged under the brine.
  4. Cover with an airlock or a safely venting lid. Keep at 70–75°F for about 3–4 weeks. Check regularly and keep the cucumbers below the brine.
  5. Once fully fermented, refrigerate. Discard any batch that becomes slimy, mushy, moldy, or smells unpleasant.

REMEMBER

Fermentation weights help keep cucumbers under the surface; floating vegetables are more prone to spoilage. These are salt-fermented pickles, not quick vinegar pickles. Use the listed salt/water/vinegar ratios.

Ridiculously Easy Kimchi

CABBAGE + FRUIT-PEPPER PASTE + WHATEVER VEGETABLES YOU LIKE

Ingredients: 1 head Napa or ordinary cabbage; plenty of pickling salt for presalting; ¼ cup gochugaru (Korean red pepper flakes); 1 apple or pear; optional garlic and ginger; any other vegetables (carrots, onions, radishes are favorites).

TIME

About 30–45 min active prep. Eat fresh or ferment 1–2 days cool, then refrigerate and try at ~day 6.

Instructions

  1. Wash cabbage and cut medium-thick. Rub salt into all pieces. Rest at least 30 minutes while preparing the other ingredients; leave longer if needed to wilt. Rinse thoroughly and drain.
  2. Blend the apple or pear with optional garlic and ginger. Stir in ¼ cup gochugaru to make the sauce.
  3. Cut extra vegetables however you prefer—matchsticks work well. Mix with cabbage, then massage the sauce in to coat everything.
  4. Pack tightly in a clean jar with headroom; press vegetables below released juices. Use an airlock or vented lid.
  5. Eat immediately as fresh kimchi, or ferment about 1–2 days at ~68°F, then refrigerate. Flavor keeps developing; try after about six days.

EASY SWAPS

Regular cabbage is fine. Fish sauce is optional and deliberately omitted here. Ordinary crushed red pepper is hotter than gochugaru: start with ~1 tbsp instead of ¼ cup and adjust.

SAFETY

Salt, acidification, and refrigeration matter; garlic, ginger, and pepper paste do not make extended room-temperature storage safe. For a longer room-temperature ferment, use tested salt ratios and verify acidity (pH ≤ 4.6).

Simple Sushi-Style Pickled Ginger

THIN GINGER + SWEET RICE VINEGAR • NO FERMENTATION

Ingredients: About 4 oz fresh ginger; ½ cup unseasoned rice vinegar (preferably 5% acidity); ¼ cup sugar; ½ tsp salt.

PICKLE TIME

Overnight in the refrigerator (about 1 day).

Instructions

  1. Peel ginger (a spoon works well) and slice paper-thin with a peeler or mandoline.
  2. Briefly boil the slices for 1–2 minutes to mellow the sharpness; drain well. Older, tougher ginger may need another minute.
  3. Warm vinegar, sugar, and salt just until dissolved. Put ginger in a clean jar and pour the warm mixture over it to cover.
  4. Cool briefly, cover, and refrigerate overnight. Keep refrigerated and use a clean utensil to serve.

NOTES

Young ginger may naturally turn pink; mature ginger usually stays pale yellow. This is a refrigerator pickle, not shelf-stable canning. Best enjoyed within a few weeks.

Fermentation Reminders

• Keep vegetables fully submerged under the brine or released juices; use a clean fermentation weight for sauerkraut and pickles.

• Leave room for liquid and gas expansion. Use an airlock or a venting lid, not an unattended sealed jar.

• Use the salt ratio for the particular vegetable, not one universal ratio for everything.

• Refrigerate when ready; none of these recipes are shelf-stable canning instructions.

Homemade Flour & Ingredient Shortcuts

Plain brown rice flour works for many everyday recipes. When you want an all-purpose substitute, use the following easy blend. This book’s recipes are already written for gluten-free flour; the advice about substitution refers to adapting recipes from elsewhere.

ONE PART OF EACH • FOUR PARTS TOTAL, OR FIVE WITH BUCKWHEAT

  1. Measure equal parts of each ingredient (for example, one cup each). If skipping buckwheat, there are four equal parts; with it, there are five.
  2. If grinding the grains yourself, grind them as finely as possible. Mix all flours and the starch thoroughly.

XANTHAN GUM

For some baked goods, add xanthan gum as a binder when the recipe or flour blend does not already contain one. For quick breads, about ½ tsp per cup of flour is a useful starting point; bread may need more. Pancakes, coatings, and thickening may need none. Too much gum can make food gummy.

WEIGHT VS. VOLUME

When converting a conventional recipe, replacing flour BY WEIGHT sometimes works better than cup-for-cup because flour densities differ. But even by weight, expect to adjust liquid or binders. Easy Magic Shell

CHOCOLATE CHIPS + COCONUT OIL • MICROWAVE 30 SEC

Ingredients

A handful of chocolate chips; a generous dollop of coconut oil. (Starting point: about ½ cup chips + 1 tbsp coconut oil.)

Instructions

  1. Put chocolate chips and coconut oil in a small microwave-safe dish.
  2. Microwave about 30 seconds. Stir until smooth; if needed, microwave a few seconds more and stir again. Do not scorch the chocolate.
  3. Pour over cold ice cream. It hardens into a shell as it cools.

REUSE

It will probably solidify in the dish. Microwave about 20 seconds, stir, and pour again. Add a little coconut oil if the shell seems too thick.

Texas Sheet Cake

350°F • ABOUT 20 MIN • 10 × 15-INCH RIMMED PAN

Ingredients

CAKE: 2 cups gluten-free 1:1 baking flour (with binder), 2 cups sugar, 1 tsp baking soda, ½ tsp salt, ½ cup sour cream, 2 eggs, 1 cup butter, 1 cup water, 5 tbsp unsweetened cocoa.

ICING: 6 tbsp milk, 5 tbsp cocoa, ½ cup butter, 4 cups powdered sugar, 1 tsp vanilla, optional 1 cup chopped walnuts.

Instructions

  1. Preheat the oven to 350°F; grease a 10 × 15-inch rimmed jelly-roll pan.
  2. Mix flour, sugar, baking soda, and salt. Beat in sour cream and eggs.
  3. In a saucepan, melt the cake butter with water and cocoa; bring just to a boil and remove from heat. Cool slightly, then mix into the batter.
  4. Spread in the pan and bake about 20 minutes, until a toothpick inserted in the center comes out clean. GF flour may alter the time slightly.
  5. While it bakes, heat icing butter, milk, and cocoa in a saucepan just to a boil. Remove from heat and stir in powdered sugar and vanilla; add walnuts if desired.
  6. Pour the warm icing onto the still-warm cake and spread promptly before it sets.

SOURCE

Adapted from Carolyn Herbert, “Texas Sheet Cake,” Allrecipes: https://www.allrecipes.com/recipe/23056/texas-sheet-cake-v/. The original uses wheat flour; this gluten-free substitution is a practical starting point, not a personally tested conversion.

PAN NOTE

Use the specified shallow 10 × 15-inch pan when possible. A deeper 9 × 13-inch pan will need a longer bake.

Easy Crispy Rice Treats

STOVETOP: ABOUT 10 MIN • SET: ABOUT 15 MIN • 9 × 13 PAN

Ingredients

1 stick butter; 1 bag marshmallows (usually 10 oz); roughly 6–8 cups gluten-free crispy rice cereal (add more if still well coated). Optional: lots of cinnamon, splashes of vanilla, chocolate chips.

Instructions

  1. Grease a 9 × 13-inch glass pan with butter or cooking spray.
  2. In a large pot over medium to medium-low heat, melt butter and marshmallows together. Stir constantly so nothing burns. Add cinnamon and vanilla if desired.
  3. When almost melted, stir in the cereal a little at a time, coating it thoroughly. Use as much of the box as the marshmallow mixture will comfortably hold.
  4. Mix in chocolate chips if desired (they may melt unless the mixture has cooled a little).
  5. Transfer to the pan. Press into an even layer using a buttered spatula or lightly greased hands once cool enough to touch.
  6. Let set about 15 minutes before cutting.

CEREAL NOTE

One 10-oz marshmallow bag usually coats about 6 cups cereal; a whole large box may make drier treats. Regular branded Rice Krispies can contain barley malt—choose a labeled gluten-free crispy rice cereal.

Apples Three Ways: Filling, Crisp, or Pie

ONE SIMPLE APPLE MIXTURE • EAT AS-IS OR BAKE TWO DIFFERENT WAYS

Basic apple filling

About 6 apples (peeled or unpeeled, cored, sliced), ¼–½ cup sugar or brown sugar, cinnamon to taste, optional squeeze of lemon and pinch of salt.

Instructions

  1. Toss apples with sugar, cinnamon, and optional lemon. That is the filling. You can eat it just like that! For warm filling, sauté with a little butter until soft (about 8–12 minutes).
  2. To make a CRISP: Put the filling in a greased 8 × 8 or 9 × 9-inch baking dish. Mix ½ cup gluten-free oats + ½ cup gluten-free flour + ½ cup brown sugar + ½ stick softened butter into crumbs. Scatter on top. Bake at 375°F for about 30–40 minutes, until apples are tender and top is golden.
  3. To make a PIE: Put the filling in a purchased gluten-free 9-inch pie crust. Stir 2 tbsp cornstarch into the apples first to thicken their juices. Add a second purchased crust on top if desired; cut steam slits. Bake at 375°F for about 50–65 minutes, until the filling bubbles and the crust is browned. Protect crust edges with foil if they darken too quickly.

REMEMBER

Filling is apples + sugar + cinnamon. Crisp adds a crumb topping. Pie adds a store-bought crust and a little cornstarch. Follow the purchased crust’s package directions if they differ.

TEXTURE

Let pie cool for about 2 hours before cutting so it holds together. For apple crisp, a scoop of ice cream is an obvious improvement. Choose gluten-free oats and crust.
